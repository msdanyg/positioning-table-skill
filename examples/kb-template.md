# Competitive KB template — one file per competitor

> Copy this template into your competitive knowledge base (e.g. `competitive-kb/<competitor>.md`) and keep one file per competitor. The positioning-table skill reads these files as its source of competitive facts — the deeper and fresher the file, the better the table. Why a knowledge base and not a battle card: [Kill the Battle Card](https://www.cmoconfessions.com/method).

---

## [Competitor name]

**Last reviewed:** YYYY-MM-DD · **Reviewed by:** [name]
*(Facts decay. A claim checked eight months ago is a lead, not a fact.)*

### What they claim

Their public positioning, in their words — pricing page, homepage, analyst briefings, sales decks you've seen. One bullet per claim, each with a source and date:

- "[Claim as they state it]" — [source: URL or artifact], [date checked]

### Documented gaps

Where reality falls short of the claim — anchored to evidence, never to vibes. One bullet per gap:

- [The gap] — [source: their docs / trust page / integration list / win-loss interview / analyst note], [date]

### Feature-level differences

The technical detail a rep can't improvise: specific capabilities, limits, architecture choices, integration depth (native connector vs. CSV import, hosting regions, API surface). Anchored to their documentation wherever possible.

- [Capability]: theirs: [fact + source] · ours: [fact]

### Where they are strong

Write this down honestly — the table concedes real strengths and repositions rather than attacking them. A rep surprised by a competitor's strength loses the room.

- [Strength] — [evidence]

### Open questions

What you don't know yet. These feed the positioning table's Unknowns list.

- [Question] — [suggested owner: PMM / win-loss / partner check]
